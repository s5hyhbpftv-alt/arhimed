## Day 1

### Problem 1

Let ![Image 1: $n$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/7/4/174fadd07fd54c9afe288e96558c92e0c1da733a.png) be an integer greater than ![Image 2: $1$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/d/c/e/dce34f4dfb2406144304ad0d6106c5382ddd1446.png). For which real numbers ![Image 3: $x$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/2/6/e/26eeb5258ca5099acf8fe96b2a1049c48c89a5e6.png) is ![Image 4: \[\lfloor nx\rfloor-\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{\lfloor kx\rfloor}{k}\]](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/d/1/c/d1c5ce64959e8d0ec178ac668ec7f8a42130fb97.png) maximal, and what is the maximal value that this expression can take? Note: ![Image 5: $\lfloor z\rfloor$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/0/f/10f3a5f1bb872894c1dfdef6cc4c0be00307ddf3.png) denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to ![Image 6: $z$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/b/1/3/b13f21416d84e13708696f34dea81026cda583c9.png).

[Solution](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php?title=2026_USAMO_Problems/Problem_1 "2026 USAMO Problems/Problem 1")

### Problem 2

Annie is playing a game where she starts with a row of positive integers, written on a blackboard, each of which is a power of ![Image 7: $2$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/4/1/c/41c544263a265ff15498ee45f7392c5f86c6d151.png). On each turn, she can erase two adjacent numbers and replace them with a power of ![Image 8: $2$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/4/1/c/41c544263a265ff15498ee45f7392c5f86c6d151.png) that is greater than either of the erased numbers. This shortens the row of numbers, and she continues to take turns until only one number remains. Annie wins the game if the final remaining number is less than ![Image 9: $4$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/7/c/c7cab1a05e1e0c1d51a6a219d96577a16b7abf9d.png) times the sum of the original numbers. Is it always possible for Annie to win, regardless of the starting row of numbers?

[Solution](https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php?title=2026_USAMO_Problems/Problem_2 "2026 USAMO Problems/Problem 2")

### Problem 3

Let ![Image 10: $ABC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/e/2/a/e2a559986ed5a0ffc5654bd367c29dfc92913c36.png) be an acute scalene triangle with no angle equal to ![Image 11: $60^\circ$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/2/9/f/29fa0a9949f018e121b2093216dd5db13d3abef0.png). Let ![Image 12: $\omega$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/4/d/54d7d48553f4d9e7ab418118607ea324cbfddfda.png) be the circumcircle of ![Image 13: $ABC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/e/2/a/e2a559986ed5a0ffc5654bd367c29dfc92913c36.png). Let ![Image 14: $\triangle_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/0/e/30e6bc175e2a4591ad2167ca267da27cb065adca.png) be the equilateral triangle with three vertices on ![Image 15: $\omega$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/4/d/54d7d48553f4d9e7ab418118607ea324cbfddfda.png), one of which is ![Image 16: $B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/f/5/ff5fb3d775862e2123b007eb4373ff6cc1a34d4e.png). Let ![Image 17: $\ell_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/f/f/0ffb8fae480f25fbfb60dd7b827ea225e81b7f02.png) be the line through the two vertices of ![Image 18: $\triangle_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/0/e/30e6bc175e2a4591ad2167ca267da27cb065adca.png) other than ![Image 19: $B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/f/5/ff5fb3d775862e2123b007eb4373ff6cc1a34d4e.png). Let ![Image 20: $\triangle_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/2/f/f2fd23bec5a71f3bc7898f703bd261ea0d1ea96f.png) and ![Image 21: $\ell_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/8/5/3856ff459e8e0f763e995fae8680728ba30beef3.png) be defined analogously. Let ![Image 22: $Y$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/e/5/ce58e4af225c93d08606c26554caaa5ae32edeba.png) be the intersection of ![Image 23: $AC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/a/1/7/a179ff2638e4799cadd820db205c2beff6299ce9.png) and ![Image 24: $\ell_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/f/f/0ffb8fae480f25fbfb60dd7b827ea225e81b7f02.png), and let ![Image 25: $Z$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/e/a/3ead47fb9fb4a4c273feee398f72ff2a09702b84.png) be the intersection of ![Image 26: $AB$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/7/e/57ee5125358c0606c9b588580ddfa66f83e607b7.png) and ![Image 27: $\ell_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/8/5/3856ff459e8e0f763e995fae8680728ba30beef3.png).

Let ![Image 28: $N$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/c/9/fc97ef67268cd4e91bacdf12b8901d7036c9a056.png) be the midpoint of minor arc ![Image 29: $BC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/6/c/5/6c52a41dcbd739f1d026c5d4f181438b75b76976.png) on ![Image 30: $\omega$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/4/d/54d7d48553f4d9e7ab418118607ea324cbfddfda.png). Let ![Image 31: $\mathcal{R}$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/2/b/f2b686e282112ee7338ebaf3427727ac8141c10f.png) be the triangle formed by ![Image 32: $\ell_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/f/f/0ffb8fae480f25fbfb60dd7b827ea225e81b7f02.png), ![Image 33: $\ell_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/8/5/3856ff459e8e0f763e995fae8680728ba30beef3.png), and the tangent to ![Image 34: $\omega$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/4/d/54d7d48553f4d9e7ab418118607ea324cbfddfda.png) through ![Image 35: $N$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/c/9/fc97ef67268cd4e91bacdf12b8901d7036c9a056.png). Prove that the circumcircle of ![Image 36: $AYZ$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/6/2/0/620bb149eedce7c8570c99aafa2a8834e51fcc17.png) and the incircle of ![Image 37: $\mathcal{R}$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/2/b/f2b686e282112ee7338ebaf3427727ac8141c10f.png) are tangent.

### Problem 4

A positive integer ![Image 38: $n$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/7/4/174fadd07fd54c9afe288e96558c92e0c1da733a.png) is called solitary if, for any nonnegative integers ![Image 39: $a$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/7/d/c7d457e388298246adb06c587bccd419ea67f7e8.png) and ![Image 40: $b$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/8/1/3/8136a7ef6a03334a7246df9097e5bcc31ba33fd2.png) such that ![Image 41: $a+b=n$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/6/e/4/6e42302f9dd0afe4a7cd50ac2115f72bee849423.png), either ![Image 42: $a$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/7/d/c7d457e388298246adb06c587bccd419ea67f7e8.png) or ![Image 43: $b$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/8/1/3/8136a7ef6a03334a7246df9097e5bcc31ba33fd2.png) contains the digit ![Image 44: $1$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/d/c/e/dce34f4dfb2406144304ad0d6106c5382ddd1446.png) when written in base ![Image 45: $10$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/c/6/fc606f7f1e530731ab4f1cc364c01dc64a4455ee.png). Determine, with proof, the number of solitary integers less than ![Image 46: $10^{2026}$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/4/2/042b44ed762ce8a071b63c5b94d3adccd573ded3.png).

### Problem 5

Let ![Image 47: $ABC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/e/2/a/e2a559986ed5a0ffc5654bd367c29dfc92913c36.png) be a triangle. Points ![Image 48: $D$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/9/f/f/9ffb448918db29f2a72f8f87f421b3b3cad18f95.png), ![Image 49: $E$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/a/2/fa2fa899f0afb05d6837885523503a2d4df434f9.png), and ![Image 50: $F$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/a/0/5/a055f405829e64a3b70253ab67cb45ed6ed5bb29.png) lie on sides ![Image 51: $BC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/6/c/5/6c52a41dcbd739f1d026c5d4f181438b75b76976.png), ![Image 52: $CA$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/e/6/5/e657259c0995d2c36d46744bb4aa81471e6575ec.png), and ![Image 53: $AB$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/7/e/57ee5125358c0606c9b588580ddfa66f83e607b7.png), respectively, such that ![Image 54: \[\angle AFE=\angle BDF=\angle CED.\]](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/9/f/7/9f79a0be62330f80052b8447229914474e5e6903.png) Let ![Image 55: $O_A$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/4/d/8/4d8aa423de2031c09aa06a1687767414dbb8120d.png), ![Image 56: $O_B$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/e/0/0e0af44578003bbd64382d00ed07ed2e33099284.png), and ![Image 57: $O_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/b/e/2/be2b7d9af12c551508264ff8ed054503c4e1b890.png) be the circumcenters of triangles ![Image 58: $AFE$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/3/7/8/378fc67ebfe29ddad36d7bab7b5851c10738c166.png), ![Image 59: $BDF$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/9/3/b/93b93a2dc17d4cf5e91cf1edaa02b2a16f690e42.png), and ![Image 60: $CED$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/d/c/5/dc5309b25510f426ca00c957623bd0396c79e4c3.png), respectively. Let ![Image 61: $M$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/d/1/5d1e4485dc90c450e8c76826516c1b2ccb8fce16.png), ![Image 62: $N$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/f/c/9/fc97ef67268cd4e91bacdf12b8901d7036c9a056.png), and ![Image 63: $O$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/5/1/d/51da37d984564162c87710ca27bea422f657fb73.png) be the circumcenters of triangles ![Image 64: $ABC$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/e/2/a/e2a559986ed5a0ffc5654bd367c29dfc92913c36.png), ![Image 65: $DEF$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/2/e/9/2e9e8b2c23e0545ef6de81f0ea578173e9ab7a64.png), and ![Image 66: $O_AO_BO_C$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/b/0/5/b05c844d6d1a5d916d2a6a78d3a38ea13c28d1b8.png), respectively. Prove that ![Image 67: $OM=ON$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/0/0/c/00c790407cf1ea6e6b8dda1272b335c932ae1dea.png).

### Problem 6

Let ![Image 68: $a$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/7/d/c7d457e388298246adb06c587bccd419ea67f7e8.png) and ![Image 69: $b$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/8/1/3/8136a7ef6a03334a7246df9097e5bcc31ba33fd2.png) be positive integers such that ![Image 70: $\varphi(ab+1)$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/b/d/2/bd2e9fb001e4bae91c4a1af297fc7af1bcd469f8.png) divides ![Image 71: $a^2+b^2+1$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/5/7/157b1248bad25e9621cdf712aa24411ec4698a84.png). Prove that ![Image 72: $a$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/c/7/d/c7d457e388298246adb06c587bccd419ea67f7e8.png) and ![Image 73: $b$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/8/1/3/8136a7ef6a03334a7246df9097e5bcc31ba33fd2.png) are Fibonacci numbers.

Note: Euler’s totient function ![Image 74: $\varphi(n)$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/6/3/d/63d7e990c9ebf7adc93ddd233b1239e5e4c0eef3.png) is the number of positive integers less than or equal to ![Image 75: $n$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/7/4/174fadd07fd54c9afe288e96558c92e0c1da733a.png) that are relatively prime to ![Image 76: $n$](https://latex.artofproblemsolving.com/1/7/4/174fadd07fd54c9afe288e96558c92e0c1da733a.png).
